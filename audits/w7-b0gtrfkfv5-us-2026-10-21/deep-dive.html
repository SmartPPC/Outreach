Still fix it, as a quick win. In Makeup Sets it could earn a Best Seller badge, which it can't win against home décor products. Its rank would be measured against the right products, and Amazon would match it better on broad "advent calendar" searches. The Holiday Haul has the same problem.

3. Content and mobile (from our first audit): not the problem

The listing passes every mobile check. It has 7 zoomable images at 2000px, a brand video, 10 phone-friendly A+ images, the brand, product and size within the first 80 characters of the title, short bullets, and 5,300+ reviews at 4.4★. There's one seller (W7 Makeup) with a 99% Buy Box. Time and money should go into traffic quality, not content.

Working

  • Ads plus the price cut lifted organic sales about 6× a day (Sep ~7/day → Oct ~41/day without an ad).
  • Auto and competitor-product targeting buy calendars cheapest (~$22).
  • Beauty searches: the calendar takes 8–15% of purchases there.

Not working

  • Every SP sale costs $29.30 in ads on a $27.19 product. Across all sales, ads cost ~$18.40 per calendar.
  • Spend almost tripled in two weeks ($4.0k → $11.6k a week), and the latest full week returned only $0.95 of ad sales per $1.
  • The "General terms" campaign: $42.60 per calendar on broad and off-target searches.

6. Actions, in priority order

  1. Cut the "General terms" campaign from $1,000 to about $200 a day. Keep only exact-match terms that sell below the target cost per calendar (action 2). Add negatives: needoh, nee doh, squishy, dumpling, chocolate, lego, fishing, halloween, for adults, amazon beauty advent calendar, 25 days of beauty.
    Saves about $800 a day. This campaign is 28% of calendar spend at 139% ACOS.
  2. Set a target ad cost per calendar and bid to it. We suggest about $10–12 (ACOS ~35–45%), confirmed against W7's product cost plus FBA fees. Lower bids on any target running above it for 7+ days.
    Ad cost per sale is $22–43 today, against a $27 price.
  3. Move the budget into the beauty terms where the calendar wins, at the top of search. Use exact match on beauty advent calendar 2026, beauty advent calendar, makeup advent calendar 2026, make up advent calendar 2026, makeup advent calendar, teen advent calendar 2026, advent calendar for teen girls, with a +50–100% top-of-search adjustment (0% today).
    It ranks organically #3–10 here and takes 8–15% of purchases. A second slot at the top of the page doubles its presence where shoppers already pick it.
  4. Add a Spanish-language ad group for calendario de adviento, calendario de adviento maquillaje, calendario de adviento 2026.
    109k + 47k searches a month, organic #9–10, and 9.8% of purchases from 3.4% of clicks. Usually cheaper clicks.
  5. Add competitor-brand keyword targeting for rival calendars. Today the only competitor campaign targets rival product pages (ASINs). There's no keyword campaign on searches like sephora advent calendar 2026 (18.8k a week, 2.8% purchase share), ulta advent calendar 2026 (7.9%), elf advent calendar 2026 (3.2%) and essence advent calendar 2026. Bid exact match, at the top of search.
    These shoppers are already looking for a beauty calendar, and the typical rival costs $60–95 against W7's $27. That's a strong price-led conquest message.
  6. Tighten the $1,400/day Sponsored Brands video to beauty terms and the brand search, then cap it until it hits the target cost per calendar.
    The biggest single budget, at 80% ACOS.
  7. Request the category change to Beauty & Personal Care › Makeup › Makeup Sets through a Seller Support case. Cite the UK listing and competitors' categories, and include the Holiday Haul.
    Low effort. Opens up the Best Seller badge and a cleaner rank. Not the main growth lever.
  8. Hold the price at $27.19 for now. Test $29.99 once ad waste is cut.
    It's half the market price and wins on beauty searches. A small rise on organic and cheaper paid sales may protect margin without losing many sales. Check weekly in Search Query Performance.

Bottom line. The listing, the price and the reviews are winning: shoppers choose it over e.l.f., Sephora and Ulta when they see it. The fix is to stop paying for broad, kids and squishy searches, and to put that money at the top of the beauty searches where it already sells. Do that now, before the late-November peak.
