Sales, conversion and ad spend review

21 October 2026

October has been a big month for the calendar. It's selling around 9× more a day than in September, and on beauty searches shoppers are picking it over e.l.f., Sephora and Ulta. The catch is what the ads are costing: roughly $29 in clicks for every $27 calendar sold through an ad. Most of the upside is in redirecting that spend, and the category move should add to it.

The spend hasn't been wasted. Together with the price cut, it has pushed organic sales from about 7 a day to about 41. Auto and competitor product-page targeting are the cheapest ways the account is buying sales.

But every campaign is paying more than $20 in ads to sell a $27 calendar. Spend nearly tripled over two weeks, and in the latest full week each $1 of spend brought back $0.95 in sales.

6Actions

  1. Bring the general "advent calendar" campaign down from $1,000 to around $200 a day. "Advent calendar 2026" does bring in orders, so keep it on exact match with a low bid. Add negatives for needoh, squishy, dumpling, chocolate, lego, for adults, amazon beauty advent calendar and amazon advent calendar.
    That frees up about $800 a day from the one campaign that's clearly losing money.
  2. Put that budget behind the green searches, at the top of the page. Use exact match on beauty advent calendar 2026, makeup advent calendar 2026, make up advent calendar 2026, luxury beauty advent calendar 2026, makeup advent calendar and advent calendar 2026 makeup/beauty. Add the teen and women's searches too, such as advent calendar 2026 teen girls, makeup advent calendar 2026 for teens and advent calendar 2026 women. Set a top-of-search adjustment of 50–100%.
    These are the searches where it already ranks on page one and wins more than its share of sales.
  3. Go after rival calendars, starting with their product pages. The competitor product-page campaign is one of the cheaper ones at about $23 per calendar. Widen it to the best-selling Sephora, Ulta, e.l.f., L'Oréal and Revolution calendars. Bidding on those brand names in search is worth a small test, no more: Ulta searches convert at about the market rate, but Sephora and e.l.f. searches convert at roughly half.
    These shoppers are already looking at a $60–95 calendar. W7's is $27.
  4. Add the Spanish searches calendario de adviento maquillaje and calendario de adviento.
    Around 42k searches a week. It's already on page one for both, and converts well.
  5. Agree a target ad cost per calendar. We'd suggest $10–12, depending on margin, then pull bids back on anything running above it.
    It's anywhere from $22 to $43 at the moment.
  6. Narrow the Sponsored Brands video ($1,400 a day) down to the green searches and W7's own brand searches. Keep a small exact-match bid on w7 advent calendar.
    It's the largest single budget. People searching for W7 by name buy it 70–90% of the time.
  7. Raise a Seller Support case to move the calendar, and the Holiday Haul, into Makeup Sets.
    It should get shown to more beauty shoppers, convert better on the broad searches, and become eligible for a Best Seller badge.

In short: the calendar itself is doing well. The product, the price and the reviews are all working. What needs to change is where the ad money goes. Less on broad and off-target searches, more at the top of the beauty searches and on rival calendars' pages. Ideally this is sorted, along with the category move, before the late-November peak.

A few caveats. Organic sales are estimated by taking ad-attributed sales away from total sales. The last few days will still pick up some ad sales, because Amazon credits orders for 7 days after a click. Some of the September-to-October growth is just the season starting.
