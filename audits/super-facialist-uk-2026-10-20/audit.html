Smart PPC · Amazon audit

Super Facialist

amazon.co.ukPrepared 2026-10-21

Super Facialist's best-sellers already have strong listings. The upside is in where the ad budget goes, and in a Rosehip range that's set up in a way that holds its cleansing balm back.

Priorities

What I would do first

  1. catalogue

    Rosehip Cleansing Balm: move it out of Day Care and give it its own variation family

    The balm (part of a ~£17k/mo family) is listed under Moisturisers › Day Care and shares one variation family with a day cream, night cream and facial oil, so it competes against moisturisers rather than other cleansing balms. Moving it to Cleansing Balms and splitting the family by product type is the single biggest fix for the product the Brand Store leads with.

    What we saw
    • B0D6BWBYY2 category leaf: Day Care (Keepa)
    • Parent B0F8R55N8X live children: Cleansing Balm, SPF15 Day Cream, Overnight Night Cream, Rose Miracle Facial Oil
    • Family revenue ~£17,394/mo; balm 882 units/mo at £14 (Jungle Scout)
    • 11 sellers on the balm listing; Amazon holds the Buy Box
  2. advertising

    Stop paying for 'super' and unrelated searches across all best-sellers

    Every best-seller has a sponsored ranking on 'super' (12.5k searches/mo, sponsored #1 for the Cleansing Oil), and each one also shows ads on terms with nothing to do with it, from 'claudia winkleman' and 'no 7 foundation' on the balm to 'claritin' on the blemish wash. That looks like auto or broad campaigns drifting. Negatives plus a search-term review would free budget for the terms below.

    What we saw
    • 'super' (12,490/mo): sponsored #1 Cleansing Oil, #2 Salicylic Wash, #4 Micro Polish, #10 Cleansing Balm
    • Cleansing Balm sponsored on: st eval, bare anatomy rosemary and rice water spray, claudia winkleman, no 7 foundation, witch hazel liquid
    • Cleansing Oil sponsored on: bioderma micellar water, garnier micellar water, isoclean, nothora
    • Salicylic Face Wash sponsored on: sudosalve, claritin, mario badescu
    • Source: Jungle Scout keywords by ASIN, sponsored rank with no organic rank
  3. advertising

    Vitamin C Cleansing Oil: take the hero into generic cleanser searches

    The Cleansing Oil is the engine (~£39.7k/mo, 5,300 reviews, 4.5★) and already ranks on page 2–3 for big generic terms with no ad support: 'face cleanser' (22k/mo, organic #36) and 'facial cleanser' (8k/mo, #30). Exact-match Sponsored Products at the top of search on those terms is the quickest way to grow its best product.

    What we saw
    • B00KZYWQLM: 5,315 units/mo, £7.47 (Jungle Scout), 4.5★ from 5,303 reviews
    • face cleanser: 22,136/mo, organic #36, no sponsored rank, CPC ~£0.96
    • facial cleanser: 7,989/mo, organic #30, no sponsored rank, CPC ~£0.75
  4. advertising

    Salicylic Blemish Face Wash: advertise on 'face wash' and acne terms

    This ~£12.9k/mo family has 2,000+ reviews but is sponsored on only 15 terms, some of them unrelated. It ranks organically for 'face wash' (82k/mo, #45), 'salicylic acid' (36k/mo, #48) and 'acne face wash' (#25) with no ads, at low CPCs (~£0.24–0.54).

    What we saw
    • B07K1Z7Q7V: 1,592 units/mo at £6.30, 4.4★ from 2,065 reviews
    • face wash: 82,259/mo, organic #45, CPC ~£0.39
    • salicylic acid: 36,360/mo, organic #48, CPC ~£0.24
    • acne face wash: 4,093/mo, organic #25, CPC ~£0.54
  5. catalogue

    Quick wins: tidy up two more mixed product families

    The Salicylic wash shares a variation family with a toner, and the Vegan Collagen Night Cream shares one with a cleanser, moisturiser and serum. Splitting them by product type gives each its own reviews, rank and the right category.

    What we saw
    • Parent B0H2BDYNF4: face wash + toner
    • Parent B0D9LRH3ZN: night cream + cleanser + moisturiser + serum
    • B00IEU6OF8 titled 'Superfacialist…' rather than 'Super Facialist'

Amazon Marketing Cloud

Path to purchase and repeat buying

Repeat-purchase signals: high (Subscribe & Save on 70% of ASINs, multipacks 0%, consumable category).

What we expect AMC to show

  • Every live product offers Subscribe & Save in a consumable category, so repeat buyers are probably a large share of Cleansing Oil revenue.
  • Searches for 'super facialist cleansing oil' show more than ten competitor ads, so some shoppers looking for the brand are likely buying a rival's cleanser instead.

Questions we would answer in week one

  1. How many Cleansing Oil buyers come back, and after how many days?
  2. What share of ad-driven sales are new-to-brand on generic cleanser terms versus brand terms?
  3. Which product do new customers buy first, and what do they buy next?
  4. How many shoppers who search the brand name end up buying another brand?
Method

How this was put together

Built from public marketplace data (Keepa, Jungle Scout, DataForSEO, Amazon catalogue and bid recommendations) on 2026-10-21. We have not seen this brand's Seller Central or advertising account, so spend and budget figures are estimates.

Budget model: search volume × 12% of searches producing an ad click × target sponsored share of voice by tier × CPC benchmark, plus 15% for remarketing.
